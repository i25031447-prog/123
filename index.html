About Guangzhou

The City of Rams

A brief introduction to one of China's greatest historic ports and most modern metropolises.

Guangzhou, the capital of Guangdong Province, is one of China's most vibrant metropolises with a history spanning over 2,200 years. Known as the "City of Rams," it has served as a major port on the Maritime Silk Road and remains a crucial hub for international trade.

As the birthplace of Cantonese cuisine, Guangzhou is recognized as a UNESCO City of Gastronomy. The city's culinary scene ranges from humble street-side noodle stalls to Michelin-starred restaurants, all united by an obsession with fresh ingredients and delicate flavors.

Beyond food, Guangzhou captivates visitors with its incredible diversity of experiences — ancient temples, Lingnan architecture and a waterfront that glows with light after sunset.

Location

Where Is Guangzhou?

Guangzhou sits in the south of Guangdong Province, at the heart of the Pearl River Delta. It is less than an hour from Hong Kong by high-speed rail and shares the Delta with Shenzhen, Foshan and Macau.

Must-See Attractions

Six places that define Guangzhou — ancient parks, iconic towers and the river that ties it all together.

Landmark

Canton Tower

The iconic 600-meter tower dominates the skyline. Visit the 488-meter observation deck, ride the world's highest Ferris wheel, or enjoy the LED light show after dark.

Park

Yuexiu Park

The largest park in downtown Guangzhou, home to the famous Five Rams Sculpture, lush gardens, Zhenhai Tower and the Guangzhou Museum.

Nature

Baiyun Mountain

Known as the "Green Lung" of Guangzhou, Baiyun Mountain offers miles of hiking trails and panoramic city views from the summit.

Heritage

Shamian Island

A preserved enclave of 19th-century European colonial architecture, with tree-lined boulevards, historic churches and charming cafes.

Monument

Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall

An octagonal building built in 1931 honoring the father of modern China, surrounded by calm gardens and hosting cultural performances.

Nightlife

Pearl River Night Cruise

Sail past illuminated bridges and the glowing Canton Tower — the perfect way to unwind and see the city from a new perspective.

Taste the Soul of Cantonese Cuisine

Fresh ingredients, gentle seasoning and a devotion to texture — these are the dishes that built a UNESCO City of Gastronomy.

Dim Sum

Har Gow & Siu Mai

Crystal shrimp dumplings and pork-and-shrimp dumplings — the iconic duo of morning tea and a social ritual in Guangzhou.

Roast

Roast Goose

Crispy golden skin over tender meat marinated with five-spice — the famous Panyu roast goose is considered the finest in China.

Noodles

Wonton Noodles

Springy egg noodles topped with plump shrimp and pork wontons in a clear, savory broth — a lesson in balance and texture.

Soup

Slow-Simmered Soup

Old-fire soup is simmered for hours with herbs and seasonal ingredients — Cantonese cooks believe good soup heals the body.

Roast

Char Siu (BBQ Pork)

Honey-glazed pork roasted till the edges caramelize perfectly. Every roast shop in Guangzhou guards its own secret recipe.

Breakfast

Rice Rolls (Chang Fen)

Silky steamed rice noodles rolled around shrimp or beef and drizzled with sweet soy sauce — the classic Guangzhou breakfast.

Signature Dish

Char Siu (BBQ Pork)

Glossy, caramelized Char Siu is a Cantonese masterpiece. The pork is marinated in honey, soy sauce and five-spice, then roasted until the edges caramelize perfectly. Every roast shop in Guangzhou guards its own recipe.

Try it on its own, with rice, or inside a steamed Char Siu Bao.

Breakfast Staple

Rice Rolls (Chang Fen)

Thin, silky steamed rice noodles rolled around shrimp, pork or beef and drizzled with sweet soy sauce. The best versions are steamed to order in small neighborhood shops right before your eyes.

Showcasing the Cantonese mastery of delicate steaming techniques.

Experience Authentic Cantonese Culture

Six living traditions you can still witness in Guangzhou today.

Tradition

Yum Cha — Morning Tea

Locals spend mornings at teahouses selecting dim sum from bamboo steamers, pouring tea for elders first as a sign of respect.

Festival

Spring Flower Market

Every Lunar New Year the city blooms with flower markets — buying orchids for the home is a tradition for good luck.

Performance

Cantonese Opera

Guangzhou is the cradle of Yueju, a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage blending singing, martial arts and acrobatics.

Festival

Awakened Lion Dance

The Southern Lion Dance originated in Guangzhou — the famous pole-jumping routine expresses joy and curiosity through the lion's features.

Race

Dragon Boat Racing

Every Dragon Boat Festival, teams race long wooden boats across the river to the thunder of drums — a tradition over 2,000 years old.

Lifestyle

Riverside Evening Walk

As the sun sets, locals stroll the Pearl River greenway, watching the skyline light up and sipping tea by the water.

Getting There

Baiyun International Airport connects to 200+ cities. The Airport Express reaches downtown in 40 minutes; high-speed rail links to Hong Kong in 1 hour.

Getting Around

The 16-line metro is the best way to explore. Buses cost 2 CNY and taxis start at 12 CNY. Biking the Pearl River greenway is a great option.

Best Season

October to December is cool and dry. Spring is bright but rainy; summer is hot and humid; winter is mild at 10–20°C.

Money & Payment

Cash is accepted, but mobile payment apps are the norm. ATMs and currency exchange are available at the airport and major hotels.

Language

Cantonese is the local language, but Mandarin is widely spoken. Many young locals and service staff speak basic English.

What to Pack

Light breathable clothing, sun protection and a compact umbrella — Guangzhou can see sudden afternoon showers year-round.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to visit?

October to December offers the most comfortable weather. Spring and early summer are warmer and more humid but beautiful and green.

Do I need a visa?

Many travellers can now enjoy visa-free entry for short stays. Always check the latest rules for your nationality before booking.

How do I get around?

The metro is fast, cheap and bilingual. For short trips, ride-hailing apps and taxis are easy; the Pearl River greenway is perfect for cycling.

What should I not miss?

Canton Tower at night, an early-morning dim sum meal, Shamian Island's architecture and a Pearl River cruise to end the day.
